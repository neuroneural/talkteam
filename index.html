Brain Causality

Causal $\ne$ Measurement Timescale

$\mathcal{G}^1 \approx 100\text{ ms}$
$\mathcal{G}^u \stackrel{?}{\longleftarrow} \mathcal{G}^1$
$\mathcal{H} \approx 2\text{ s}$
What causal inferences can be made when measurement is $20\times$ slower?

Representation

Dynamic Bayesian Network (DBN)
  • Causally sufficient
  • First-order Markov
  • No edges within a time step
Representation and diagram from causaltalk. No isochronal edges means there are no directed edges within one time slice. Causal sufficiency and first-order Markov dynamics are assumptions at the causal timescale.

Original vector figure imported from causaltalk. Each column compresses the temporal graph above into a graph over variable identities below. Black arrows show lagged directed relationships, orange bidirected edges represent common causes at unobserved intermediate times.

Original simple-loops figure from causaltalk. The graph contains loops of lengths 4, 5 and 2. Their greatest common divisor is 1. Loop structure helps explain how an undersampled graph changes as the rate varies.

Search Tree

Plis, S., Danks, D., & Yang, J. (2015). Mesochronal structure learning. UAI 2015.
Original search-tree illustration from causaltalk. Each level explains an observed edge. Propagating a candidate through time exposes conflicts with the measured graph. A conflict permits backtracking and pruning the whole subtree.
Plis, S., Danks, D., & Yang, J. (2015). Mesochronal structure learning. UAI 2015.
Original runtime_demo.png from causaltalk. Left: ratios of options relative to edge-wise search for eight- and ten-node graphs. Right: distributions of runtime for eight-node graphs. Deliberate Undersampling

Solovyeva, Danks, Abavisani and Plis (CLeaR 2023), Figure 6. The gain is the base-10 logarithm of the ratio of the equivalence-class size for one rate to the size after intersecting two adjacent rates. Colours count graphs. Combining measurements at multiple rates can shrink the equivalence class. The formula and original paper caption are outside the displayed crop. The upper illustration distinguishes monotonic and non-monotonic steps, so a further undersampling step alone need not shrink an equivalence class.
